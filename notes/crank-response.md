# Crank response at small angles

I compared crank turns at two fixed fin angles, 0° and 1°. At both angles, more crank turns produced nearly proportional increases in fin rotations, and no scrapes were recorded.

| Fin angle | Crank turns | Fin rotations | Scrapes |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 0° | 2 | 3.0 | 0 |
| 0° | 4 | 6.1 | 0 |
| 0° | 4 | 5.9 | 0 |
| 0° | 4 | 6.0 | 0 |
| 0° | 8 | 12.0 | 0 |
| 0° | 8 | 12.1 | 0 |
| 1° | 2 | 3.0 | 0 |
| 1° | 4 | 6.0 | 0 |
| 1° | 8 | 11.9 | 0 |

The response stays close to 1.5 fin rotations per crank turn at both angles. Doubling the crank turns roughly doubles the rotations: for example, at 0°, two turns gave 3.0 rotations and eight gave about 12; at 1°, the corresponding readings were 3.0 and 11.9.

The small differences among readings—such as 5.9 to 6.1 rotations for four turns at 0°—are minor beside that near-proportional pattern. In these trials, changing the angle from 0° to 1° did not produce a clear change in rotation rate or scrapes. This comparison covers only those two angles; whether the pattern holds at other angles remains unknown.
