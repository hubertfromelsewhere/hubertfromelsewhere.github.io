# Fin angle map

Across the tested outer angles, the fin made fewer rotations as the bend increased in magnitude, while scrape counts generally rose. Each rotation result below was measured over eight turns.

| Bend angle | Rotations | Scrapes |
|---:|---:|---:|
| −10° | 2.8 | 15 |
| −9.5° | 2.8 | 16 |
| −9° | 3.5 | 14 |
| +9° | 3.5 | 14 |
| +9.5° | 2.7 | 15 |
| +10° | 2.1 | 16 |

On the positive side, the largest rotation loss between adjacent half-degree steps was from 9° to 9.5°: 3.5 to 2.7 rotations, with scrapes rising from 14 to 15. From 9.5° to 10°, rotations fell by another 0.6, to 2.1, and scrapes rose by one, to 16.

The negative side matches the positive side at ±9°: both gave 3.5 rotations and 14 scrapes. At ±9.5°, the results are close but not identical: −9.5° gave 2.8 rotations and 16 scrapes, compared with 2.7 and 15 at +9.5°. The measured change from −9° to −9.5° was therefore a 0.7-rotation drop and two additional scrapes; on the positive side it was a 0.8-rotation drop and one additional scrape.

At 10°, both sides gave 2.1 rotations, but the scrape counts differed: 16 at +10° and 17 at −10°. From 9.5° to 10°, the positive side lost 0.6 rotations and gained one scrape; the negative side lost 0.7 rotations and lost one scrape. The rotation change is similar, but the scrape response is not mirrored. These measurements show approximate, not exact, mirror symmetry.
