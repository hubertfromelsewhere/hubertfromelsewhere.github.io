# Fin angle rates

I compare rotations and audible scrapes per crank turn. Each rate below is calculated from the recorded total divided by the number of turns. The repeated trials make it possible to see how much the results vary at a setting.

| Fin angle | Turns per trial | Rotations per turn | Scrapes per turn |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 0° | 8 | 1.50 | 0 |
| 5° | 8 | 1.08–1.10 | 0.375 |
| 6° | 8 | 0.925–0.938 | 0.75 |
| 8° | 8 | 0.600 | 1.375 |
| 9° | 8 | 0.425–0.438 | 1.75 |
| 9.5° | 8 | 0.338–0.350 | 1.875–2.00 |
| 10° | 8 | 0.263 | 2.125 |

At 0°, four trials produced 11.8, 12.0, 12.1, and 12.2 rotations, with no scrapes. That is a narrow spread: about 1.48–1.53 rotations per turn. At 5°, two trials gave 8.6 and 8.8 rotations, with 3 scrapes each. At 6°, two trials gave 7.5 and 7.4 rotations, with 6 scrapes each. These paired readings are close.

At 8°, both trials gave 4.8 rotations and 11 scrapes. At 9°, the rotation totals were 3.4 and 3.5, with 14 scrapes each. At 9.5°, the totals were 2.8 and 2.7 rotations, with 16 and 15 scrapes. At 10°, both trials gave 2.1 rotations and 17 scrapes. The repeats at each of these settings are close, though the 9.5° scrape count varies by one.

The pattern is clear across the measured settings: increasing the fin angle accompanies fewer rotations and more scrapes. The decline is especially large between 6° and 8° (about 0.93 to 0.60 rotations per turn), and continues through the closely spaced 8°–10° measurements. The 9.5° result lies between 9° and 10°; these observations suggest a gradual change over that interval, not a measured abrupt threshold. They do not establish the curve between every tested setting or beyond the tested range.
