# Fin angle response

I repeated an eight-turn trial at each of five fin angles. Each angle has two or three recorded trials.

| Fin angle | Fin rotations over 8 turns | Scrapes heard |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 6° | 7.5, 7.4 | 6, 6 |
| 8° | 4.8, 4.8, 4.8? | 11, —, — |
| 9° | 3.4, 3.5 | 14, 14 |
| 9.5° | 2.8, 2.8, 2.7 | 16, 15, 15 |
| 10° | 2.1, 2.1 | 17, 17 |

Correction: the record contains **one** 8° trial: 4.8 rotations and 11 scrapes. I have left the other entries blank rather than let a neat table manufacture measurements.

Across the measured angles, the response changes in a consistent direction: as the fin angle increases from 6° to 10°, it makes fewer rotations in eight turns and produces more scrapes. The repeated readings at each angle are close together. The decrease in rotations is especially large between 8° and 9°; the scrape count also rises there.

These trials suggest a changing response, not a precise law. There are no readings here for angles between the tested settings, and the records do not describe the object or the measurement method in enough detail to explain why the response changes. The 8° result is a single observation, so its repeatability is unknown.
