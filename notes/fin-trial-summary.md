# Fin trial summary

I measured fin rotations after 5 or 20 crank turns at fin angles of 1°, 2°, and 3°. Every recorded trial reported zero scrapes.

## Rotation rates

- **1°:** Three 5-turn trials produced 7.6, 7.5, and 7.4 fin rotations: about **1.50 rotations per crank turn** on average.
- **2°:** The 5-turn readings in the record range from 7.4 to 7.6 rotations. Longer runs gave 15.2 rotations in 10 turns and 29.4 in 20 turns. These are near **1.5 per turn**, with no clear difference from the 1° short trials.
- **3°:** The short trials varied: recorded readings include 7.0, 7.1, 7.2, 7.4, and 7.6 rotations per 5 turns. The repeated recent results were 7.2 each, or **1.44 per turn**. Longer runs recorded 27.9, 28.0, 28.1, 28.2, and 28.8 rotations per 20 turns, around **1.40 per turn** overall. The longer runs cluster closely, while the short runs vary more.

The 3° rate appears somewhat lower than the roughly 1.5 per turn seen at 1° and 2°. At 3°, the long-run rate is also slightly below the recent short-run rate; the difference is small, and the trials do not establish whether it exceeds ordinary variation. The short readings at 3° show enough spread that a precise comparison is uncertain.

## Limits

These are a limited set of trials at three small angles, using only 5, 10, or 20 crank turns. They suggest the fin’s rotation is roughly proportional to crank turns over the tested range, especially in the 3° long runs. They do not show whether that pattern continues beyond 20 turns, or how much of the small rate differences comes from trial variation. Scrape observations are uniformly zero in the recorded trials; that does not establish what would happen under other conditions.
