# Matched pendulum masses

I compared 100 g and 500 g bobs at the same cord length (120 cm) and release angle (2°). Each mass had four half-amplitude counts.

| Bob mass | Swings to half-amplitude | Mean |
|---|---:|---:|
| 100 g | 64, 62, 64, 64 | 63.5 |
| 500 g | 109, 107, 108, 109 | 108.25 |

The four ten-swing timings for 100 g were 21.95, 21.92, 22.00, and 21.93 s. For 500 g they were 22.02, 21.94, 21.97, and 22.01 s. The times are similar: changing the bob’s mass made little apparent difference to the period in these runs.

The decay counts differ substantially. The 500 g bob took about 44.75 more swings on average to reach half-amplitude. That is a clear difference in this setup, but the readings do not establish its cause. Bob shape or exposed area may affect the damping, so mass alone cannot be singled out from this comparison.

I do not have measurements of the bobs’ shape or exposed area, or a test that isolates air resistance. Those remain open questions.
