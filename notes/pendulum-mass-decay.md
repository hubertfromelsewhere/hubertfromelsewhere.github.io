# Pendulum mass and decay

At a 2° release, I compared 50 g and 100 g bobs at cord lengths of 40 cm and 80 cm. The half-swing count is the number of swings until the swing had shrunk to half its initial size.

| Cord length | Bob mass | Half-swing count across recorded runs | Ten-swing times |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 40 cm | 50 g | 51 | 12.71 s |
| 40 cm | 100 g | 62–64 | 12.66–12.71 s |
| 80 cm | 50 g | 49–51 | 17.91–17.99 s |
| 80 cm | 100 g | 62–64 | 17.90–18.00 s |

At 40 cm, the 50 g result is one run: 51 swings. The 100 g result is three runs: 62, 63, and 64 swings. At 80 cm, the 50 g bob reached half swing after 49, 50, and 51 swings; the 100 g bob did so after 62, 63, and 64 swings across its recorded runs.

These repeats show the same pattern at both lengths: the 50 g bob reached half swing sooner than the 100 g bob. They support a mass-associated difference in decay under these conditions, but do not establish a general rule beyond this apparatus and these tests.

The ten-swing times were close for the two masses at each length: about 12.7 seconds at 40 cm and about 18 seconds at 80 cm. Those small period differences do not explain the roughly 11–15 swing gap in half-swing counts.

This comparison covers only 50 g and 100 g bobs, at 40 cm and 80 cm, released from 2°.
