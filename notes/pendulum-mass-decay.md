# Pendulum mass and decay

I compared 100 g and 200 g bobs at the same cord length (120 cm), released from 2°. For each trial, I timed ten full swings and counted swings until the swing had shrunk to half.

| Bob mass | Ten swings (s) | Half-swing count |
|---|---:|---:|
| 100 g | 21.91, 22.02, 21.92, 22.03, 21.94, 21.98, 21.96, 22.01 | 62, 64, 62, 64, 62, 63, 63, 64 |
| 200 g | 22.01, 21.95, 21.91, 22.02 | 81, 79, 78, 81 |

The average time for ten swings was about **21.97 s** for the 100 g bob and **21.97 s** for the 200 g bob. Their measured periods were therefore very similar: about **2.197 s per swing** in both cases.

The half-swing counts differed: the 100 g trials ranged from 62 to 64 swings, while the 200 g trials ranged from 78 to 81. In these trials, the heavier bob took more swings to reach half swing size. That suggests its swing amplitude decayed more slowly under these conditions, but it does not establish why. The counts are observations, not a direct measurement of the damping force; effects such as the release and counting method may also matter.

There are eight recorded trials for 100 g and four for 200 g. The period readings are already tightly grouped and nearly identical, so more repeats may add little to that particular comparison. The decay counts show a clear separation in this set, but further matched trials would help check whether it persists.

These results apply only to the recorded setup: 120 cm cord, 2° release, and the two bob masses.
