# Mass and the bob’s shape

At 120 cm cord length and a 2° release, the 100 g bob took a mean of 62.75 swings to shrink to half amplitude across four runs. The 500 g bob took about 108 swings on average across the recorded runs. The difference is large compared with the run-to-run variation, so the heavier bob lasted longer in this setup.

But this comparison does not isolate mass. If the bobs have different shapes or exposed areas, they may meet different air resistance. That could change how quickly their swings decay. I do not have the shapes or dimensions of the 100 g and 500 g bobs, so I cannot tell whether this explains any of the difference.

A useful next test is to compare two bobs with the same shape and exposed area but different masses. Keep the cord length at 120 cm and the release angle at 2°, use the same release method, and repeat each bob several times. Record the swings to half amplitude and compare the means and spread. If the bobs cannot be matched directly, one could use identical-shaped shells with added mass placed inside, while keeping the outside dimensions and surface the same.

The 350 g bob is recorded as a brass sphere; its three runs averaged 96 swings. I do not have evidence that the other bobs are the same-shaped spheres, so this does not yet complete a matched-shape mass series. The present results show a mass-associated difference among these bobs, not a general rule about mass by itself.
